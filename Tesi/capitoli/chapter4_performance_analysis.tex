\chapter{Performance Analysis, Optimization, and Experimental Evaluation}
\label{chap:performance_analysis}

This chapter presents the experimental evaluation, systematic parametric tuning, and consolidated validation of the proposed Centralized Integer Linear Programming (ILP) Orchestrator~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}. While Chapter~\ref{chap:ilp_architecture} established the formal mathematical optimization model and its discrete-event simulation architecture, deploying exact centralized scheduling in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellations introduces fundamental operational trade-offs~\cite{denby2019orbital, survey_orbital_edge_2023}. In particular, centralized optimization must balance the queuing latency introduced by task accumulation in ingress buffers against the physical dynamics of dynamic inter-satellite topologies and strict application deadlines~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, zhong2025leo}.

To address these challenges, this chapter follows a progressive two-stage tuning methodology concluded by a final validation of the resulting configurations against the pre-optimization baseline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Macro-Tuning of Ingress Batching ($BS \times BT$):} We explore an exhaustive design space of 28 batching configurations across increasing traffic arrival rates ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$) and deadline relaxation thresholds ($\Delta D$) using baseline default weights. This isolates the queuing latency bottleneck and identifies the viable operational batching policies.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Micro-Tuning of Objective Weights ($\gamma \times \alpha$):} Focusing on the viable batching policies under heavy load ($\lambda = 8\text{ req/s}$ and $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$), we perform a cross-sensitivity analysis between the primary objective weight ($\alpha$) and the orbital sunset penalty ($\gamma$) to maximize feasibility while preventing the assignment of tasks to satellites nearing orbital eclipse.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Consolidated Validation:} With the optimal objective weights determined, we validate the selected configurations across the full arrival rate spectrum ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$), directly comparing their performance against the pre-optimization baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Simulation Environment and Methodological Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

The experimental campaign was carried out using an event-driven discrete-event simulation engine implemented in Python with \texttt{SimPy}~\cite{simpy2023}, interfaced with the \texttt{PuLP} mathematical programming library~\cite{pulp2011} and solved via the open-source \texttt{HiGHS} branch-and-cut solver~\cite{huangfu2018highs}. Numerical telemetry and array manipulations were processed using \texttt{NumPy}~\cite{harris2020numpy} and \texttt{SciPy}~\cite{virtanen2020scipy}, while performance distributions and diagnostic curves were generated using \texttt{Matplotlib}~\cite{hunter2007matplotlib}. The simulator models orbital kinematics~\cite{vallado2006sgp4}, optical Inter-Satellite Links (ISLs)~\cite{chen2021isl, chaudhry2021laser}, dynamic ground-to-satellite line-of-sight contact windows, and heterogeneous onboard Satellite Edge Nodes (SENs)~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

\subsection{Constellation Topology and Scenario Parameters}
\label{subsec:topology_params}

The orbital network is modeled as a regional Walker-Delta LEO constellation operating at an altitude of $600\text{ km}$ with an inclination of $53^\circ$~\cite{walker1984satellite}. Satellites are interconnected via full-duplex optical ISLs characterized by nominal transmission bandwidths $B_{u,v}$ and propagation delays $\tau_{u,v}$ computed dynamically from orbital positions~\cite{chaudhry2021laser}. A designated Master in the operational region collects offloaded task requests forwarded by Ground Users (GUs) through regional Ground Access Points ($AP_{global}$)~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

Workload demands and request dimensions are parameterized through three profiling vectors, consistent with recent benchmarking literature~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Workload Distribution Vector $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (\beta_G, \beta_C, \beta_{CD})$:} Dictates the proportion of Generic tasks, CPU-intensive tasks, and CPU + Data-intensive tasks. In the baseline batching and tuning experiments, the default multi-service profile is set to $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0.40, 0.35, 0.25)$~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Payload Vector $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_M, \alpha_L, \alpha_V)$:} Governs the distribution of input/output dataset dimensions across Medium ($1\text{--}5\text{ MB}$), Large ($5\text{--}20\text{ MB}$), and Very Large ($20\text{--}50\text{ MB}$) files, parameterized as $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (0.30, 0.50, 0.20)$~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, leyva2023satellite}.
    \item \textbf{Batch Task Data Vector $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{data} = (\gamma_L, \gamma_V)$:} Controls delay-tolerant background transfers, fixed to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{data} = (0.70, 0.30)$~\cite{denby2019orbital}.
\end{itemize}

Candidate edge nodes are assigned operational battery reserves of $E_i \in \{40, 80\}\text{ kJ}$ per orbital pass~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, peng2025blocked}. The request arrival rate $\lambda$ varies systematically from $2\text{ req/s}$ (low-load, mean inter-arrival time of $0.50\text{ s}$) up to $10\text{ req/s}$ (heavy-load stress regime, mean inter-arrival time of $0.10\text{ s}$)~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

\subsection{Two-Tier Optimization Methodology}
\label{subsec:methodology_roadmap}

Directly testing all permutations of batching thresholds, objective weights, and network loads would require thousands of simulations and obscure the physical origins of performance bottlenecks. We therefore decouple the optimization process:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Architectural Decoupling:} Ingress batching ($BS, BT$) governs the static waiting time of tasks in the coordinator queue \textit{before} the ILP model is instantiated. Conversely, objective function weights ($\alpha, \gamma$) guide the mathematical branch-and-bound trade-offs \textit{during} solver execution~\cite{wolsey1998integer, boyd2004convex}.
    \item \textbf{Sequential Tuning:} By first sweeping $BS \times BT$ under default weights, we isolate the feasible batching configurations that do not cause queue-induced deadline collapse. Next, we tune the objective weights ($\alpha, \gamma$) under heavy stress on these selected batching archetypes. Finally, we benchmark the optimized configurations against the pre-tuning baseline across the entire workload spectrum.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Macro-Tuning: Exploration and Selection of Batching Policies}{Macro-Tuning: Exploration and Selection of Batching Policies (BS x BT)}}
\label{sec:batching_exploration}

In a centralized orchestrator, incoming requests are buffered in ingress queues until either the Batch Size ($BS$) or the Batch Timeout ($BT$) threshold is triggered. This introduces a fundamental operational trade-off: larger batches aggregate broader spatial-temporal context, enabling the ILP model to optimize joint multi-task routing and node allocation, but prolonged buffering directly consumes application deadline margins~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

To isolate the queuing latency bottleneck and establish the viable operating envelope, a parametric sweep of 28 configurations was executed across arrival rates $\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$ under both Energy Mapping and Time Mapping, evaluated across deadline relaxation levels $\Delta D \in \{10\%, 20\%, 30\%\}$.

\label{subsec:sr_batch_sweep}

The empirical curves highlight five key operational dynamics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Peak Low-Load Throughput and Stress Convergence of Instantaneous Dispatch ($BT = 0.0\text{ s}$):} Enforcing an immediate dispatch policy ($BS=20, BT=0.0\text{ s}$, uppermost curve) maximizes throughput under light-to-moderate traffic, achieving $\approx 92\%\text{--}93\%$ success at $\lambda \le 3\text{ req/s}$ and maintaining $\approx 89\%$ at $\lambda=4\text{ req/s}$.     \item \textbf{Controlled Small-Batching Dynamics and High-Load Convergence ($BS$-Sweep):} Small aggregation quotas ($BS \in \{1, 2\}$ with $BT=2.0\text{ s}$) deliver robust performance across the arrival spectrum. While assembling larger batches under low traffic causes severe degradation due to prolonged timeout dwell times ($\approx 67\%$ for $BS \le 2$ vs. $\approx 44\%$ for $BS=10$ at $\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$ under Energy Mapping), all batch size configurations converge to a narrow band ($\approx 38\%\text{--}48\%$) at peak stress ($\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$). Under heavy load, the rapid inter-arrival window ($\approx 100\text{ ms}$) fills batch quotas within $0.2\text{--}1.0\text{ s}$, well before the $2.0\text{ s}$ timeout expiration.
    \item \textbf{Systematic Degradation under High-Timeout Regimes ($BT \ge 0.4\text{ s}$):} Relying on generous buffer timeouts ($BT \ge 0.4\text{ s}$, lower orange and yellow curves in the right panels) consistently degrades performance across all traffic regimes. Buffer residency directly consumes the deadline budget, capping success rates below $50\%$ even under light load ($\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$) and deteriorating to $30\%\text{--}35\%$ under peak load ($\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$).
\end{itemize}

The failure diagnostics reveal three structural properties of the constellation orchestrator:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Elimination of Algorithmic and Contact Window Drops:} Across all tested configurations and arrival rates, rejections due to \textit{ILP Infeasible}, \textit{Sunset}, and \textit{No Server Found} remain at $0.0\%$. This confirms that the ILP mathematical formulation preserves solvability, the contact window predictor successfully avoids satellites nearing orbital sunset, and ingress ground stations consistently maintain multi-satellite visibility. Task rejections are strictly categorized into temporal expirations (\textit{Deadline Exceeded}) or physical resource exhaustion (\textit{Insufficient CPU} and \textit{Insufficient CPU+NET}).

    \item \textbf{Load-Dependent Failure Transition in Instantaneous Dispatch ($BS = 20, BT = 0.0\text{ s}$):} 
    When buffer dwell time is structurally eliminated ($BT = 0.0\text{ s}$, outermost left bar of each arrival rate cluster in the right panels), failure causes exhibit a sharp, load-dependent transition:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Under light-to-moderate traffic ($\lambda \in [2, 4]\text{ req/s}$), task rejections due to \textit{Deadline Exceeded} (blue) are strictly suppressed ($< 5\%$). Rejections are overwhelmingly driven by physical resource scarcity: \textit{Insufficient CPU+NET} (orange) accounts for $\approx 70\%\text{--}75\%$ of drops, while \textit{Insufficient CPU} (green) accounts for the remaining $\approx 20\%\text{--}25\%$. Without coordinator buffering, tasks fail directly because spatial link bandwidth or onboard compute slots are unavailable upon arrival.

As arrival frequencies exceed the processing rate of individual satellites, uncoordinated single-task dispatch causes localized onboard execution queues ($W_{cpu,i}$) to expand rapidly. Consequently, even without ingress buffering delay, cumulative processing queues push execution past task deadlines, causing \textit{Deadline Exceeded} to surge to $\approx 55\%\text{--}58\%$ at $\lambda = 6\text{ req/s}$ and reaching $\approx 80\%\text{--}85\%$ at $\lambda \in [8, 10]\text{ req/s}$, while physical capacity drops diminish below $15\%$.

    \item \textbf{Rapid Deadline Collapse in Buffered Policies ($BT \ge 0.05\text{ s}$):} 
    Introducing even a marginal buffer delay alters the failure distribution. For $BT = 0.05\text{ s}$, \textit{Deadline Exceeded} accounts for $\approx 50\%\text{--}55\%$ of drops at $\lambda = 2\text{ req/s}$, escalating to $\approx 80\%\text{--}85\%$ at $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$. For $BT \ge 0.1\text{ s}$, buffer dwell time exhausts available application slack times, making \textit{Deadline Exceeded} the primary cause of rejection ($70\%\text{--}85\%$) across all loads, while physical resource rejections contract to $15\%\text{--}30\%$.
\end{itemize}

The empirical decompositions validate the analytical response time model:
\begin{equation}
    T_{total} = T_{exec}(|R|, \lambda) + T_{net+wait}(\lambda, BS, BT)

    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:texec_bounds}
\end{equation}
When larger batches are dispatched simultaneously, multiple co-allocated tasks enter satellite CPU execution queues concurrently, driving up $T_{exec}$. This behavior is directly visible in the $BT$-sweep ($BS=20$), where expanding $BT$ from $0.0\text{ s}$ to $1.0\text{ s}$ allows more tasks to accumulate per cycle, increasing $T_{exec}$ from $\approx 0.18\text{ s}$ to $\approx 0.58\text{ s}$.

The transmission and coordinator wait time ($T_{net+wait}$, top translucent segments) governs the remaining latency:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Batch Size Sweep ($BT = 2.0\text{ s}$):} Small batch sizes limit total latency across all arrival rates ($T_{total} \approx 0.35\text{--}0.55\text{ s}$ for $BS=1$, and $\approx 0.40\text{--}0.65\text{ s}$ for $BS=2$). For large aggregations ($BS \ge 8$), ingress queuing and multi-task ISL contention maintain elevated latency across the entire spectrum, spanning $0.95\text{--}1.05\text{ s}$ at $\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$ and expanding up to $1.25\text{--}1.42\text{ s}$ at $\lambda \ge 6\text{ req/s}$.
    \item \textbf{Batch Timeout Sweep ($BS = 20$):} The timeout threshold sets an explicit upper bound on ingress queue dwell time. Instantaneous dispatch ($BT = 0.0\text{ s}$) yields the lowest latency ($T_{total} \approx 0.35\text{--}0.65\text{ s}$), closely followed by reactive dispatch ($BT = 0.05\text{ s}$, $T_{total} \approx 0.42\text{--}0.73\text{ s}$). \end{itemize}
Finally, comparing across the relaxation levels ($\Delta D \in \{10\%, 20\%, 30\%\}$) confirms that while deadline relaxation affects task admission rates, the response time profiles of successfully scheduled tasks remain invariant.

\subsection{Selection of Viable Batching Archetypes}
\label{subsec:selection_archetypes}

The parametric exploration establishes clear operational boundaries for centralized orchestration in dynamic LEO satellite networks~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, survey_orbital_edge_2023}. On the one hand, configurations relying on generous buffering thresholds ($BT \ge 0.2\text{ s}$ or $BS \ge 6$) prove structurally unviable for delay-sensitive edge services: coordinator ingress dwell times systematically consume application slack times, resulting in severe deadline-induced drop rates ($70\%\text{--}85\%$, as shown in Figures~\ref{fig:failure_causes_energy} and~\ref{fig:failure_causes_time})
and elevated end-to-end response times ($> 1.0\text{ s}$, Figures~\ref{fig:line_rt_energy} and~\ref{fig:line_rt_time})~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.
On the other hand, while pure instantaneous dispatch ($BT = 0.0\text{ s}$) eliminates coordinator buffer latency, it introduces severe architectural and scheduling limitations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Degeneration into Myopic Greedy Allocation:} Structuring the optimization problem around isolated single-task instances ($|R| = 1$) fundamentally collapses the combinatorial search space. The mathematical program degenerates into a single-request shortest-path and server selection routine, blinding the orchestrator to imminent concurrent demands. In dense arrival regimes where requests arrive within tens of milliseconds of each other, they are scheduled strictly sequentially without joint fleet awareness: the first request greedily claims the lowest-latency ISL hops and the closest edge compute cores, inadvertently forcing immediately subsequent requests onto congested multi-hop detours or causing their outright rejection due to newly saturated link capacities.
    
    \item \textbf{Localized Onboard Queue Inflation and Deadline Collapse:} Because isolated single-task scheduling lacks joint visibility across concurrent demands, the solver repeatedly steers closely arriving tasks toward the same nominally optimal Satellite Edge Nodes. Although coordinator ingress buffering is eliminated ($T_{wait} = 0$), this uncoordinated dispatch rapidly congests destination node execution queues ($W_{cpu,i}$). As arrival rates exceed the processing capacity of individual satellites, the cumulative queuing delay onboard the spacecraft pushes overall task completion beyond strict application deadlines. This structural lack of multi-task coordination explains the steep throughput collapse observed under unbuffered dispatch at peak load ($\approx 41\%$--$42\%$ success at $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$, Figures~\ref{fig:line_sr_energy} and~\ref{fig:line_sr_time}).
\end{itemize}

To harness the multi-task optimization potential of the centralized ILP formulation while maintaining strict temporal feasibility, an effective batching policy must strike an operational balance: it must accumulate enough requests to enable joint combinatorial scheduling, yet flush the queue rapidly enough to preserve application deadline margins.

We therefore select two complementary batching archetypes that capture these operational trade-offs for subsequent objective weight micro-tuning:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reactive Fast-Dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$):} 
    This policy operates as a rate-limiting micro-batcher with an aggressive $50\text{ ms}$ timeout. Because the inter-arrival window across the evaluated arrival spectrum ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$) ranges between $100\text{ ms}$ and $500\text{ ms}$, the timeout triggers almost continuously, dispatching requests either individually or in tightly coupled burst pairs ($|R| \in \{1, 2\}$). By enforcing an explicit 50~ms ceiling on ingress delay, it caps end-to-end response times below 0.73~s (Figures~\ref{fig:line_rt_energy} and~\ref{fig:line_rt_time}) and prevents uncoordinated single-task allocation by coalescing sub-millisecond arrival bursts into a unified mathematical program, significantly reducing intermediate ISL contention and localized node congestion compared to unbuffered dispatch.

    \item \textbf{Controlled Small-Batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$):} 
    This policy establishes the minimal non-trivial combinatorial optimization horizon ($|R|=2$) with a fallback timeout of $2.0\text{ s}$. At operational loads ($\lambda \ge 4\text{ req/s}$), the batch size threshold is fulfilled within $0.10\text{--}0.25\text{ s}$, triggering execution well before the timeout can expire. This provides the ILP solver with a joint optimization window across pairs of competing requests, enabling spatial load balancing~\cite{tang2003loadbalancing}
    (e.g., allocating complementary CPU-intensive and Data-intensive tasks to disjoint SENs and non-overlapping ISL paths). This pairwise coupling bounds deadline-induced failures to $\le 10\%$ at medium-to-high loads (Figures~\ref{fig:failure_causes_energy} and~\ref{fig:failure_causes_time})
    while maintaining compact response times ($T_{total} \le 0.65\text{ s}$, Figures~\ref{fig:line_rt_energy} and~\ref{fig:line_rt_time}).
\end{enumerate}

% SECTION 4.3: CROSS-SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS (GAMMA x ALPHA)

\section{\texorpdfstring{Micro-Tuning: Multi-Objective Weight Calibration under Stress ($\gamma \times \alpha$)}{Micro-Tuning: Multi-Objective Weight Calibration under Stress (gamma x alpha)}}
\label{sec:cross_sensitivity}

Having isolated the viable batching configurations ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$ and $BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$), we calibrate the scalarization weights of the multi-objective function~\cite{boyd2004convex}:
\begin{equation}
    \min Z = \begin{cases}
        \alpha Z_{time} + \beta Z_{energy} + \gamma Z_{sunset}, & \text{if primary objective is Time} \\
        \alpha Z_{energy} + \beta Z_{time} + \gamma Z_{sunset}, & \text{if primary objective is Energy}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:multi_obj_evaluation}
\end{equation}
where $\beta = 1.0$ serves as the secondary objective normalization, the primary objective weight is evaluated across $\alpha \in \{10, 50, 1000\}$, and the orbital sunset deterrent penalty is evaluated across $\gamma \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 1000, 5000, 10000\}$. Note that these scalar weights are distinct from the workload profiling vectors defined in Section~\ref{subsec:topology_params}. The cross-sensitivity experiment was conducted under heavy stress regimes ($\lambda = 8\text{ req/s}$ and $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$), comparing both Dijkstra-Time and Dijkstra-Energy link routing metrics~\cite{dijkstra1959note, icdcs_satellite_2025}.

\label{subsec:cross_sr}

The empirical trends indicate:

\section{Summary of Findings and Architectural Readiness}
\label{sec:summary_findings}

The empirical evaluation and progressive tuning campaign yield four foundational conclusions regarding centralized ILP orchestration in LEO satellite edge networks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ingress Queuing Dwell Time is the Dominant Latency Bottleneck:} 
    Pure onboard processing duration on the Satellite Edge Nodes remains tightly bounded and stable ($T_{exec} \approx 0.22\text{--}0.32\text{ s}$ under calibrated configurations). System throughput collapse under elevated arrival rates is driven primarily by buffer residence times during batch aggregation in the ingress queues ($T_{wait}$), which systematically consume application deadline slack before optimization and forwarding can take place.

    \item \textbf{Necessity of Fast-Dispatch Mechanisms:} 
    To maintain temporal feasibility in dynamic space environments, centralized orchestrators must enforce low-latency dispatch policies. Operating with aggressive timeouts ($BT \le 0.05\text{ s}$) or minimal batch aggregation boundaries ($BS \le 2$) is mandatory to suppress buffer dwell times and prevent cascade \textit{Deadline Exceeded} rejections under heavy arrival regimes.

    \item \textbf{Objective-Dependent Impact of Weight Calibration:} 
    Enforcing an aggressive primary weight ($\alpha = 1000$) provides asymmetric benefits: under \textit{Time Mapping}, it compels the branch-and-cut solver to prioritize transmission timeliness and deadline preservation, delivering up to $+19.5\%$ higher task completion rates under heavy traffic. Conversely, calibrating the orbital sunset penalty to $\gamma = 10$ eliminates eclipse-induced task drops while strictly preventing the $55\%$ path-bloat latency inflation ($> 1.05\text{ s}$) observed under excessive penalties ($\gamma \ge 1000$).

    \item \textbf{Architectural Readiness for Multi-Algorithm Benchmarking:} 
    The two fully calibrated operational archetypes—reactive fast-dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$) and controlled small-batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$), configured with $\alpha=1000$ and $\gamma=10$—define the empirically optimized operating envelope of the centralized ILP approach.
\end{enumerate}

These fully tuned centralized baselines establish the reference operating points for Chapter~\ref{chap:comparative_benchmark}, where their performance is systematically benchmarked against state-of-the-art decentralized heuristics (\textit{OrbitAware}, \textit{DTS-base}, \textit{DTS-Optimal}) and distributed hierarchical ILP formulations across heterogeneous workload profiles.
