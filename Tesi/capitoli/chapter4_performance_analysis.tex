\chapter{Performance Analysis, Optimization, and Experimental Evaluation}
\label{chap:performance_analysis}

This chapter presents the experimental evaluation, systematic parametric tuning, and consolidated validation of the proposed Centralized Integer Linear Programming (ILP) Orchestrator~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}. While Chapter~\ref{chap:ilp_architecture} established the formal mathematical optimization model and its discrete-event simulation architecture, deploying exact centralized scheduling in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellations introduces fundamental operational trade-offs~\cite{denby2019orbital, survey_orbital_edge_2023}. In particular, centralized optimization must balance the queuing latency introduced by task accumulation in ingress buffers against the physical dynamics of dynamic inter-satellite topologies and strict application deadlines~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, zhong2025leo}.

To address these challenges, this chapter follows a progressive two-stage tuning methodology concluded by an ablation validation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Macro-Tuning of Ingress Batching ($BS \times BT$):} We explore an exhaustive design space of 28 batching configurations across increasing traffic arrival rates ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$) and deadline relaxation thresholds ($\Delta D$) using baseline default weights. This isolates the queuing latency bottleneck and identifies the viable operational batching archetypes.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Micro-Tuning of Objective Weights ($\gamma \times \alpha$):} Focusing on the viable batching archetypes under heavy load ($\lambda = 8\text{ req/s}$ and $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$), we perform a cross-sensitivity analysis between the primary objective weight ($\alpha$) and the orbital sunset penalty ($\gamma$) to maximize feasibility while preventing topological path-bloat.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Consolidated Validation:} With the optimal objective weights determined, we validate the selected configurations across the full arrival rate spectrum ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$), directly comparing their performance against the pre-optimization baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Simulation Environment and Methodological Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

The experimental campaign was carried out using an event-driven discrete-event simulation engine implemented in Python with \texttt{SimPy}~\cite{simpy2023}, interfaced with the \texttt{PuLP} mathematical programming library~\cite{pulp2011} and solved via the open-source \texttt{HiGHS} branch-and-cut solver~\cite{huangfu2018highs}. Numerical telemetry and array manipulations were processed using \texttt{NumPy}~\cite{harris2020numpy} and \texttt{SciPy}~\cite{virtanen2020scipy}, while performance distributions and diagnostic curves were generated using \texttt{Matplotlib}~\cite{hunter2007matplotlib}. The simulator models orbital kinematics~\cite{vallado2006sgp4}, optical Inter-Satellite Links (ISLs)~\cite{chen2021isl, chaudhry2021laser}, dynamic ground-to-satellite line-of-sight contact windows, and heterogeneous onboard Satellite Edge Nodes (SENs)~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

\subsection{Constellation Topology and Scenario Parameters}
\label{subsec:topology_params}

The orbital network is modeled as a regional Walker-Delta LEO constellation operating at an altitude of $600\text{ km}$ with an inclination of $53^\circ$~\cite{walker1984satellite}. Satellites are interconnected via full-duplex optical ISLs characterized by nominal transmission bandwidths $B_{u,v}$ and propagation delays $\tau_{u,v}$ computed dynamically from orbital positions~\cite{chaudhry2021laser}. A designated Master in the operational region collects offloaded task requests forwarded by Ground Users (GUs) through regional Ground Access Points ($AP_{global}$)~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

Workload demands and request dimensions are parameterized through three profiling vectors, consistent with recent benchmarking literature~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Workload Distribution Vector $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (\beta_G, \beta_C, \beta_{CD})$:} Dictates the proportion of Generic tasks, CPU-intensive tasks, and CPU + Data-intensive tasks. In the baseline batching and tuning experiments, the default multi-service profile is set to $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0.40, 0.35, 0.25)$~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Payload Vector $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_M, \alpha_L, \alpha_V)$:} Governs the distribution of input/output dataset dimensions across Medium ($1\text{--}5\text{ MB}$), Large ($5\text{--}20\text{ MB}$), and Very Large ($20\text{--}50\text{ MB}$) files, parameterized as $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (0.30, 0.50, 0.20)$~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, leyva2023satellite}.
    \item \textbf{Batch Task Data Vector $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{data} = (\gamma_L, \gamma_V)$:} Controls delay-tolerant background transfers, fixed to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{data} = (0.70, 0.30)$~\cite{denby2019orbital}.
\end{itemize}

Candidate edge nodes are assigned operational battery reserves of $E_i \in \{40, 80\}\text{ kJ}$ per orbital pass~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025, peng2025blocked}. The request arrival rate $\lambda$ varies systematically from $2\text{ req/s}$ (low-load, mean inter-arrival time of $0.50\text{ s}$) up to $10\text{ req/s}$ (heavy-load stress regime, mean inter-arrival time of $0.10\text{ s}$)~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

\subsection{Two-Tier Optimization Methodology}
\label{subsec:methodology_roadmap}

Directly testing all permutations of batching thresholds, objective weights, and network loads would require thousands of simulations and obscure the physical origins of performance bottlenecks. We therefore decouple the optimization process:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Architectural Decoupling:} Ingress batching ($BS, BT$) governs the static waiting time of tasks in the coordinator queue \textit{before} the ILP model is instantiated. Conversely, objective function weights ($\alpha, \gamma$) guide the mathematical branch-and-bound trade-offs \textit{during} solver execution~\cite{wolsey1998integer, boyd2004convex}.
    \item \textbf{Sequential Tuning:} By first sweeping $BS \times BT$ under default weights, we isolate the feasible batching configurations that do not cause queue-induced deadline collapse. Next, we tune the objective weights ($\alpha, \gamma$) under heavy stress on these selected batching archetypes. Finally, we benchmark the optimized configurations against the pre-tuning baseline across the entire workload spectrum.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Macro-Tuning: Exploration and Selection of Batching Policies}{Macro-Tuning: Exploration and Selection of Batching Policies (BS x BT)}}
\label{sec:batching_exploration}

In a centralized orchestrator, incoming requests are buffered until either the Batch Size ($BS$) or the Batch Timeout ($BT$) threshold is triggered. This introduces a fundamental trade-off: larger batches enlarge the joint solution space, enabling the solver to achieve higher allocation efficiency, but prolonged buffering erodes task deadline margins~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.

To quantify this trade-off, a parametric sweep of 28 configurations was executed across arrival rates $\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$ under both Energy Mapping and Time Mapping, evaluated across deadline relaxation levels $\Delta D \in \{10\%, 20\%, 30\%\}$.

The empirical trends highlight four critical dynamics across the parameter space:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Throughput of Pure Instantaneous Dispatch ($BT = 0.0\text{ s}$):} The zero-timeout policy ($BS=20, BT=0.0\text{ s}$, uppermost dark curve) achieves the highest overall completion rate, maintaining $\approx 93\%$ success at $\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$ and remaining above $88\%$ up to $\lambda=4\text{ req/s}$. Beyond moderate load, however, it experiences a steep drop, declining to $\approx 40\%$ at $\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$ due to physical node and link contention under continuous single-task arrivals.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Small-Batching Dynamics and High-Load Convergence ($BS$-Sweep):} Configurations enforcing small aggregations ($BS \in \{1, 2\}$ with $BT=2.0\text{ s}$) or reactive timeouts ($BT=0.05\text{ s}$) demonstrate balanced performance, starting at $\approx 76\text{--}79\%$ at $\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$ and degrading monotonically to $\approx 42\text{--}47\%$ at peak traffic. Crucially, while increasing $BS$ from $1$ to $10$ causes a severe penalty at low arrival rates ($\approx 78\%$ vs. $\approx 54\%$ at $\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$ due to prolonged queue buffering), all batch size configurations converge to a narrow band ($41\%\text{--}47\%$) at $\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$, as rapid request arrivals naturally accelerate batch formation before timeout expiration.
    \item \textbf{Performance Degradation in High-Timeout Regimes ($BT \ge 0.4\text{ s}$):} Relying on generous buffer timeouts (orange and yellow curves in the right panels) systematically penalizes requests across all arrival rates. Dwell times in the ingress queue erode critical deadline margins, holding success rates below $58\%$ even under light traffic ($\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$) and collapsing to $31\%\text{--}36\%$ under stress ($\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$).
    \item \textbf{Invariance to Objective Metric, Routing Strategy, and Deadline Slackness:} The success curves for Time Mapping and Energy Mapping are virtually identical (differing by less than $2.0\%$), and Dijkstra-Energy (solid lines) matches Dijkstra-Time (dashed lines) almost perfectly across all operational points. Furthermore, expanding deadline relaxation from $\Delta D = 10\%$ to $30\%$ provides negligible throughput improvements ($<3\%$), verifying that task rejections in delayed configurations exceed deadlines by margins far wider than the relaxation delta.
\end{itemize}

The failure diagnostics reveal distinct operational regimes governed by the interaction between batch aggregation thresholds and traffic intensity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strict Capacity-Bounded Regime ($BS = 1$):} 
    When task aggregation is structurally disabled ($BS=1$), task rejections due to \textit{Deadline Exceeded} (blue) are completely suppressed ($0\%$) across all arrival rates ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$). Because requests bypass coordinator queuing entirely, drops occur exclusively from physical constellation saturation: \textit{Insufficient CPU} (green, accounting for $60\%\text{--}65\%$ of rejections) and \textit{Insufficient CPU+NET} (orange, accounting for $35\%\text{--}40\%$). A similar capacity-dominated profile is observed for $BS=2$ at moderate-to-high traffic ($\lambda \ge 4\text{ req/s}$), where deadline drops remain below $5\%$.

    \item \textbf{Load-Dependent Degradation under Instantaneous Dispatch ($BS = 20, BT = 0.0\text{ s}$):} 
    Under light traffic ($\lambda = 2\text{ req/s}$), setting $BT = 0.0\text{ s}$ successfully prevents queue-induced expiration, with \textit{Deadline Exceeded} accounting for only $\approx 8\%$ of drops while physical constraints dominate (CPU: $\approx 50\%$, CPU+NET: $\approx 42\%$). However, as traffic scales toward stress levels ($\lambda \ge 6\text{ req/s}$), cumulative onboard execution queues ($W_{cpu,i}$) expand across candidate satellites. Consequently, even without ingress batch dwell time, processing delays push completion past application deadlines, causing \textit{Deadline Exceeded} to escalate dramatically to $\approx 68\%$ at $\lambda=6\text{ req/s}$ and reaching $\approx 85\%$ at $\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$.

    \item \textbf{Prevalence of Deadline Failures in Buffered Policies ($BT \ge 0.05\text{ s}$):} 
    Introducing even a minimal buffering delay ($BT = 0.05\text{ s}$) produces noticeable deadline penalties, with \textit{Deadline Exceeded} failures already at $\approx 32\%$ at $\lambda=2\text{ req/s}$ and exceeding $80\%$ under peak load. For configurations with $BT \ge 0.1\text{ s}$, buffer residency severely erodes the remaining slack time, making \textit{Deadline Exceeded} the overwhelming failure cause ($70\%\text{--}85\%$) across the entire traffic spectrum.

    \item \textbf{Bottleneck Inversion in Batch Size Aggregation ($BS$-Sweep, $BT = 2.0\text{ s}$):} 
    In the batch size sweep, failure modes invert as traffic intensifies:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item At $\lambda = 2\text{ req/s}$, assembling large batches ($BS \ge 6$) requires waiting for the prolonged $2.0\text{ s}$ timeout, triggering massive deadline expirations ($70\%\text{--}82\%$ of total drops).
        \item At $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$, rapid inter-arrival times ($\approx 100\text{ ms}$) fill the batch before timeout expiration, drastically cutting buffer residency. As a result, \textit{Deadline Exceeded} drops contract to $10\%\text{--}20\%$, and physical bottlenecks re-emerge as the primary constraint (\textit{Insufficient CPU} $\approx 45\%\text{--}50\%$, \textit{Insufficient CPU+NET} $\approx 35\%$).
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

These stacked decompositions highlight the operational mechanics governing orchestrator latency:
\begin{equation}
    T_{total} = T_{exec}(|R|) + T_{net+wait}(\lambda, BS, BT)
    \label{eq:latency_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where onboard task execution time on the assigned SEN ($T_{exec}$) is strictly bounded by local processing and node queue residency across the active batch $|R|$ rather than the external traffic arrival rate:
\begin{equation}
    T_{exec} \approx \begin{cases}
        0.25\text{--}0.50\text{ ms}, & \text{for } BS \le 2 \text{ or } BT \le 0.05\text{ s} \\
        0.90\text{--}1.20\text{ ms}, & \text{for large batches } (BS \ge 8 \text{ or } BT \ge 0.6\text{ s})
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:texec_bounds}
\end{equation}
For any given batch configuration, the execution footprint remains remarkably stable across arrival rates ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$).
Conversely, the end-to-end response time is heavily dominated by the queuing and transmission component ($T_{net+wait}$, top translucent segments), which dictates the overall latency profile across the parameter sweeps:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Batch Size Sweep ($BT = 2.0\text{ s}$):} At low arrival rates ($\lambda = 2\text{ req/s}$), accumulating large batches ($BS \ge 8$) introduces severe buffer dwell time while waiting for subsequent requests, pushing total response time to its peak ($\approx 2.50\text{--}2.70\text{ ms}$). As $\lambda$ scales toward $10\text{ req/s}$, incoming requests arrive more frequently, accelerating batch completion and compressing the waiting component down to $\approx 1.20\text{ ms}$ ($T_{total} \approx 2.10\text{--}2.30\text{ ms}$).
    \item \textbf{Batch Timeout Sweep ($BS = 20$):} The timeout threshold directly acts as an upper bound on ingress queue residency. For aggressive timeouts ($BT \le 0.05\text{ s}$), buffer dwell time is virtually suppressed, maintaining total latency below $0.75\text{ ms}$. For $BT \ge 0.6\text{ s}$, requests linger in the ingress buffer until timeout expiration, causing $T_{net+wait}$ to expand beyond $1.30\text{ ms}$ across all load levels.
\end{itemize}
Finally, comparing routing strategies confirms that Dijkstra-Time (hatched bars) and Dijkstra-Energy (solid bars) yield nearly identical computational workloads, with Dijkstra-Time providing marginal path latency containment over multi-hop ISL transmissions.

\subsection{Selection of Viable Batching Archetypes}
\label{subsec:selection_archetypes}

The sweep results rule out all batching policies with $BT \ge 0.2\text{ s}$ or $BS \ge 8$, as queue dwell times systematically violate task deadlines. To represent the viable operational design space, we select two complementary configurations for subsequent weight calibration:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reactive Fast-Dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$):} Operates with an aggressive $50\text{ ms}$ timeout. Because inter-arrival times at $\lambda \le 10\text{ req/s}$ are $\ge 100\text{ ms}$, the timeout triggers almost continuously, dispatching 1--2 tasks per cycle and keeping buffer dwell times near zero.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Small-Batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$):} Enforces minimal task aggregation. At arrival rates $\lambda \ge 2\text{ req/s}$, the batch size threshold ($BS=2$) is reached within $0.2\text{--}0.5\text{ s}$, providing the solver with a joint optimization horizon over 2 requests without severe queue buildup.
\end{enumerate}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Micro-Tuning: Multi-Objective Weight Calibration under Stress ($\gamma \times \alpha$)}{Micro-Tuning: Multi-Objective Weight Calibration under Stress (gamma x alpha)}}
\label{sec:cross_sensitivity}

Having isolated the viable batching configurations ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$ and $BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$), we calibrate the scalarization weights of the multi-objective function~\cite{boyd2004convex}:
\begin{equation}
    \min Z = \begin{cases}
        \alpha Z_{time} + \beta Z_{energy} + \gamma Z_{sunset}, & \text{if primary objective is Time} \\
        \alpha Z_{energy} + \beta Z_{time} + \gamma Z_{sunset}, & \text{if primary objective is Energy}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:multi_obj_evaluation}
\end{equation}
where $\beta = 1.0$, the primary objective weight is evaluated across $\alpha \in \{10, 50, 1000\}$, and the orbital sunset deterrent penalty is evaluated across $\gamma \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 1000, 5000, 10000\}$. The cross-sensitivity experiment was conducted under heavy stress loads of $\lambda = 8\text{ req/s}$ and $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$, comparing both Dijkstra-Time and Dijkstra-Energy link routing metrics~\cite{dijkstra1959note, icdcs_satellite_2025}.

    \item \textbf{Decisive Impact of Primary Weight $\alpha = 1000$ in Latency-Centric Batching:} The influence of weight scaling is acutely concentrated in the batching configurations governed by latency minimization (\textit{Time $|$ $BS:2, BT:2.0$}).     \item \textbf{Optimal Sunset Trade-off Window $\gamma \in [6, 10]$:} In configurations responsive to weight calibration, peak throughput consistently concentrates within $\gamma \in [6, 10]$, achieving its maximum at $\gamma = 8$ and $\gamma = 10$. Within this range, the orbital penalty effectively discourages assignments to borderline satellites without distorting overall route efficiency.
    \item \textbf{Performance Degradation in Over-Penalized Regimes ($\gamma \ge 1000$):} When the eclipse deterrent is set to extreme values ($\gamma \ge 1000$), the success rate of $\alpha=1000$ suffers a severe decline, retreating to $\approx 52\%\text{--}55\%$ and erasing the advantage gained over smaller weights. An inflated penalty term over-constrains the optimization search space: the solver becomes overly cautious, avoiding viable satellites nearing the eclipse boundary and rejecting requests that could otherwise complete safely.
    \item \textbf{Parametric Invariance in Fast-Dispatch and Energy Mapping:} Conversely, under fast-dispatch regimes (\textit{$BS:20, BT:0.05$}) and \textit{Energy Mapping}, the success rate exhibits structural invariance across all $(\alpha, \gamma)$ combinations, remaining flat at $\approx 50\%\text{--}52\%$ at $\lambda=8\text{ req/s}$ and $\approx 40\%\text{--}42\%$ at $\lambda=10\text{ req/s}$. In fast-dispatch, the $50\text{ ms}$ timeout empties queues almost continuously, eliminating the multi-task scheduling freedom necessary for weight scalarization to take effect.
\end{itemize}

Onboard task execution time on the SEN ($T_{exec}$) remains stable and compact, consistently requiring $< 0.30\text{ ms}$ across all operational runs.
    \item \textbf{Path-Bloat and Latency Surge at $\gamma \ge 1000$:} When $\gamma$ escalates to $1000\text{--}10000$ in $BS=2$ configurations, total response time surges to $1.05\text{--}1.15\text{ ms}$ representing an approximate $55\%$ latency penalty. The stacked breakdown confirms that this surge is driven almost exclusively by the \textit{Network/Wait Time} component (which swells from $\approx 0.40\text{ ms}$ to over $0.70\text{ ms}$). To avoid the disproportionate penalty term $\Omega_i$, the ILP orchestrator detours packets through suboptimal, circuitous multi-hop ISL paths or delegates execution to distant nodes, inflating propagation delay and queue buildup.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Selection of Calibrated Objective Weights}
\label{subsec:optimal_weight_selection}

Correlating the success rate, failure distributions, and latency metrics identifies the optimal operating parameter set:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\alpha^* = 1000, \quad \gamma^* = 8 \quad (\text{with } \gamma=10 \text{ as an equivalent alternative})}
    \label{eq:optimal_tuning_parameters}
\end{equation}
This combination provides a balanced operational setting: $\alpha = 1000$ ensures that physical deadlines and compute capacity dominate solver decisions, while $\gamma = 8$ prevents assignments to satellites entering eclipse without inflating path lengths.

\section{Summary of Findings and Architectural Readiness}
\label{sec:summary_findings}

The empirical evaluation and progressive tuning campaign yield four foundational conclusions regarding centralized ILP orchestration in LEO satellite edge networks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ingress Queuing Dwell Time is the Dominant Latency Bottleneck:} 
    Pure onboard processing duration on the Satellite Edge Nodes remains tightly bounded and stable ($T_{exec} \approx 0.20\text{--}0.30\text{ ms}$ under calibrated configurations). System throughput collapse under elevated arrival rates is driven primarily by buffer residence times during batch aggregation in the ingress queues ($T_{wait}$), which systematically consume application deadline slack before optimization and forwarding can take place.

    \item \textbf{Necessity of Fast-Dispatch Mechanisms:} 
    To maintain temporal feasibility in dynamic space environments, centralized orchestrators must enforce low-latency dispatch policies. Operating with aggressive timeouts ($BT \le 0.05\text{ s}$) or minimal batch aggregation boundaries ($BS \le 2$) is mandatory to suppress buffer dwell times and prevent cascade \textit{Deadline Exceeded} rejections under heavy arrival regimes.

    \item \textbf{Objective-Dependent Impact of Weight Calibration:} 
    Enforcing an aggressive primary weight ($\alpha = 1000$) provides asymmetric benefits: under \textit{Time Mapping}, it compels the branch-and-cut solver to prioritize transmission timeliness and deadline preservation, delivering up to $+21\%$ higher task completion rates across the traffic spectrum. Conversely, calibrating the orbital sunset penalty to $\gamma = 8$ permanently eliminates task losses due to satellite eclipse transitions across all configurations, while preventing the $55\%$ path-bloat latency inflation observed under excessive penalties ($\gamma \ge 1000$).

    \item \textbf{Architectural Readiness for Multi-Algorithm Benchmarking:} 
    The two fully calibrated operational archetypes—reactive fast-dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$) and controlled small-batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$), configured with $\alpha=1000$ and $\gamma=8$—define the empirically optimized operating envelope of the centralized ILP approach.
\end{enumerate}

These fully tuned centralized baselines establish the reference operating points for Chapter~\ref{chap:comparative_benchmark}, where their performance is systematically benchmarked against state-of-the-art decentralized heuristics (\textit{OrbitAware}, \textit{DTS-base}, \textit{DTS-Optimal}) and distributed hierarchical ILP formulations across heterogeneous workload profiles.
