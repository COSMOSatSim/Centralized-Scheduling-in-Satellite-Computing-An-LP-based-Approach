\chapter{Performance Analysis, Optimization, and Experimental Evaluation}
\label{chap:performance_analysis}

This chapter presents the experimental evaluation, systematic parametric tuning, and consolidated validation of the proposed Centralized Integer Linear Programming (ILP) Orchestrator. While Chapter~\ref{chap:ilp_architecture} established the formal mathematical optimization model and its discrete-event simulation architecture, deploying exact centralized scheduling in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellations introduces fundamental operational trade-offs. In particular, centralized optimization must balance the queuing latency introduced by task accumulation in ingress buffers against the physical dynamics of dynamic inter-satellite topologies and strict application deadlines.

To address these challenges, this chapter follows a progressive two-stage tuning methodology concluded by an ablation validation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Macro-Tuning of Ingress Batching ($BS \times BT$):} We explore an exhaustive design space of 28 batching configurations across increasing traffic arrival rates ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$) and deadline relaxation thresholds ($\Delta D$) using baseline default weights. This isolates the queuing latency bottleneck and identifies the viable operational batching archetypes.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Micro-Tuning of Objective Weights ($\gamma \times \alpha$):} Focusing on the viable batching archetypes under heavy load ($\lambda = 8\text{ req/s}$ and $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$), we perform a cross-sensitivity analysis between the primary objective weight ($\alpha$) and the orbital sunset penalty ($\gamma$) to maximize feasibility while preventing topological path-bloat.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Consolidated Validation (\textit{Prima vs. Dopo}):} With the optimal objective weights determined, we validate the selected configurations across the full arrival rate spectrum ($\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$), directly comparing their performance against the pre-optimization baseline (\textit{Prima vs. Dopo}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Simulation Environment and Methodological Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

The experimental campaign was carried out using an event-driven discrete-event simulation engine implemented in Python with \texttt{SimPy}, interfaced with the \texttt{PuLP} mathematical programming library and solved via the open-source \texttt{HiGHS} branch-and-cut solver. The simulator models orbital kinematics, optical Inter-Satellite Links (ISLs), dynamic ground-to-satellite line-of-sight contact windows, and heterogeneous onboard Satellite Edge Nodes (SENs).

\subsection{Constellation Topology and Scenario Parameters}
\label{subsec:topology_params}

The orbital network is modeled as a regional Walker-Delta LEO constellation operating at an altitude of $600\text{ km}$ with an inclination of $53^\circ$. Satellites are interconnected via full-duplex optical ISLs characterized by nominal transmission bandwidths of $B_{u,v} = 1\text{ Gbps}$ and propagation delays $\tau_{u,v}$ computed dynamically from orbital positions. A designated Master satellite in the operational region collects offloaded task requests forwarded by Ground Users (GUs) through regional Ground Access Points ($AP_{global}$).

Workload demands and request dimensions are parameterized through three profiling vectors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Workload Distribution Vector $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (\beta_G, \beta_C, \beta_{CD})$:} Dictates the proportion of Generic tasks, CPU-intensive tasks, and Data-intensive tasks. In the baseline batching and tuning experiments, the default multi-service profile is set to $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0.40, 0.35, 0.25)$.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Payload Vector $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_M, \alpha_L, \alpha_V)$:} Governs the distribution of input/output dataset dimensions across Medium ($1\text{--}5\text{ MB}$), Large ($5\text{--}20\text{ MB}$), and Very Large ($20\text{--}50\text{ MB}$) files, parameterized as $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (0.30, 0.50, 0.20)$.
    \item \textbf{Batch Task Data Vector $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{data} = (\gamma_L, \gamma_V)$:} Controls delay-tolerant background transfers, fixed to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{data} = (0.70, 0.30)$.
\end{itemize}

Candidate edge nodes are assigned operational battery reserves of $E_i \in \{40, 80\}\text{ kJ}$ per orbital pass. The request arrival rate $\lambda$ varies systematically from $2\text{ req/s}$ (low-load, mean inter-arrival time of $0.50\text{ s}$) up to $10\text{ req/s}$ (heavy-load stress regime, mean inter-arrival time of $0.10\text{ s}$).

\subsection{Two-Tier Optimization Methodology}
\label{subsec:methodology_roadmap}

Directly testing all permutations of batching thresholds, objective weights, and network loads would require thousands of simulations and obscure the physical origins of performance bottlenecks. We therefore decouple the optimization process:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Architectural Decoupling:} Ingress batching ($BS, BT$) governs the static waiting time of tasks in the coordinator queue \textit{before} the ILP model is instantiated. Conversely, objective function weights ($\alpha, \gamma$) guide the mathematical branch-and-bound trade-offs \textit{during} solver execution.
    \item \textbf{Sequential Tuning:} By first sweeping $BS \times BT$ under default weights, we isolate the feasible batching configurations that do not cause queue-induced deadline collapse. Next, we tune the objective weights ($\alpha, \gamma$) under heavy stress on these selected batching archetypes. Finally, we benchmark the optimized configurations against the pre-tuning baseline across the entire workload spectrum.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Macro-Tuning: Exploration and Selection of Batching Policies ($BS \times BT$)}{Macro-Tuning: Exploration and Selection of Batching Policies (BS x BT)}}
\label{sec:batching_exploration}

In a centralized orchestrator, incoming requests are buffered until either the Batch Size ($BS$) or the Batch Timeout ($BT$) threshold is triggered. This introduces a fundamental trade-off: larger batches enlarge the joint solution space, enabling the solver to achieve higher allocation efficiency, but prolonged buffering erodes task deadline margins.

To quantify this trade-off, a parametric sweep of 28 configurations was executed across arrival rates $\lambda \in [2, 10]\text{ req/s}$ under both Energy Mapping ($w_{lat}=0, w_{en}=1$) and Time Mapping ($w_{lat}=1, w_{en}=0$), evaluated across deadline relaxation levels $\Delta D \in \{10\%, 20\%, 30\%\}$.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Selection of Viable Batching Archetypes}
\label{subsec:selection_archetypes}

The sweep results rule out all batching policies with $BT \ge 0.2\text{ s}$ or $BS \ge 8$, as queue dwell times systematically violate task deadlines. To represent the viable operational design space, we select two complementary configurations for subsequent weight calibration:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reactive Fast-Dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$):} Operates with an aggressive $50\text{ ms}$ timeout. Because inter-arrival times at $\lambda \le 10\text{ req/s}$ are $\ge 100\text{ ms}$, the timeout triggers almost continuously, dispatching 1--2 tasks per cycle and keeping buffer dwell times near zero.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Small-Batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$):} Enforces minimal task aggregation. At arrival rates $\lambda \ge 2\text{ req/s}$, the batch size threshold ($BS=2$) is reached within $0.2\text{--}0.5\text{ s}$, providing the solver with a joint optimization horizon over 2 requests without severe queue buildup.
\end{enumerate}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Micro-Tuning: Multi-Objective Weight Calibration under Stress ($\gamma \times \alpha$)}{Micro-Tuning: Multi-Objective Weight Calibration under Stress (gamma x alpha)}}
\label{sec:cross_sensitivity}

Having isolated the viable batching configurations ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$ and $BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$), we calibrate the scalarization weights of the multi-objective function:
\begin{equation}
    \min Z = \begin{cases}
        \alpha Z_{time} + \beta Z_{energy} + \gamma Z_{sunset}, & \text{if primary objective is Time} \\
        \alpha Z_{energy} + \beta Z_{time} + \gamma Z_{sunset}, & \text{if primary objective is Energy}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:multi_obj_evaluation}
\end{equation}
where $\beta = 1.0$, the primary objective weight is evaluated across $\alpha \in \{10, 50, 1000\}$, and the orbital sunset deterrent penalty is evaluated across $\gamma \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 1000, 5000, 10000\}$. The cross-sensitivity experiment was conducted under heavy stress loads of $\lambda = 8\text{ req/s}$ and $\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$, comparing both Dijkstra-Time and Dijkstra-Energy link routing metrics.

\subsection{Selection of Calibrated Objective Weights}
\label{subsec:optimal_weight_selection}

Correlating the success rate, failure distributions, and latency metrics identifies the optimal operating parameter set:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\alpha^* = 1000, \quad \gamma^* = 8 \quad (\text{with } \gamma=10 \text{ as an equivalent alternative})}
    \label{eq:optimal_tuning_parameters}
\end{equation}
This combination provides a balanced operational setting: $\alpha = 1000$ ensures that physical deadlines and compute capacity dominate solver decisions, while $\gamma = 8$ prevents assignments to satellites entering eclipse without inflating path lengths.

\section{Consolidated Validation: Pre- vs. Post-Optimization Benchmark}

\section{Summary of Findings and Architectural Readiness}
\label{sec:summary_findings}

The empirical evaluation and tuning campaign yield four foundational conclusions regarding centralized ILP orchestration in LEO satellite networks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Queuing Dwell Time is the Dominant Bottleneck:} Solver computation time remains negligible ($T_{exec} \le 0.60\text{ ms}$) across practical batch sizes. System degradation under heavy load is driven primarily by buffer residence times during batch aggregation, which trigger deadline violations before optimization begins.
    \item \textbf{Necessity of Fast Dispatch:} To maintain service feasibility, centralized orchestrators must operate with aggressive timeouts ($BT \le 0.05\text{ s}$) or minimal batch capacities ($BS \le 2$), keeping buffer residence times well below application deadlines.
    \item \textbf{Effectiveness of Objective Weight Calibration:} Enforcing a high primary performance weight ($\alpha = 1000$) focuses the solver on feasibility and deadline compliance, yielding up to $+21\%$ higher completion rates, while a moderate sunset penalty ($\gamma = 8$) eliminates connection drops due to orbital eclipse without inflating path lengths.
    \item \textbf{Architectural Readiness for Multi-Algorithm Benchmarking:} The two calibrated configurations—reactive fast-dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$) and controlled small-batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$) with $\alpha=1000$ and $\gamma=8$—represent the optimized operating envelope of the centralized ILP approach.
\end{enumerate}

These fully tuned centralized configurations provide the baseline for Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}, where their performance is benchmarked against state-of-the-art distributed heuristics (OrbitAware, DTS-base, DTS-APopt) and hierarchical ILP formulations across heterogeneous workload profiles.
