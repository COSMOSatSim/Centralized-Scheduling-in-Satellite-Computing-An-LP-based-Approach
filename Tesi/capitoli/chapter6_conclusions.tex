\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusions}

This thesis investigated the operational feasibility, performance boundaries, and systemic trade-offs of centralized mathematical optimization for computational task offloading and multi-hop routing in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite networks~\cite{survey_orbital_edge_2023, icdcs_satellite_2025}. By designing, formalizing, and implementing a Centralized Integer Linear Programming (ILP) Orchestrator coupled with an event-driven dynamic batching mechanism within a \texttt{SimPy} discrete-event simulation environment~\cite{simpy2023}, this work systematically explored the viability of orbital edge intelligence under realistic spaceborne constraints~\cite{denby2019orbital, icdcs_satellite_2025}.

This concluding chapter synthesizes the primary findings and answers the core research question formulated at the outset of the thesis. Furthermore, it details the critical physical and architectural limitations inherent to centralized space computing and outlines promising future research directions toward resilient, hybrid orbital orchestration paradigms~\cite{denby2020asplos, survey_orbital_edge_2023, tiansuan2023}.

\section{Synthesis of Contributions and Research Question Resolution}
\label{sec:concl_synthesis}

\subsection{Answering the Core Research Question}
\label{subsec:concl_rq_resolution}

In Section~\ref{sec:intro_objectives}, this thesis posed the foundational research question: \textit{To what extent can a centralized optimization approach maintain global allocation efficiency in high-density satellite networks before queuing and solver overhead cause systemic performance degradation?}

Based on the multi-tiered experimental evaluations conducted across Chapters~\ref{chap:performance_analysis} and~\ref{chap:comparative_benchmark}, the answer is clear: \textbf{the performance ceiling of centralized orbital orchestration is fundamentally architectural rather than computational}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tractability of the Solver vs. Coordinator Thrashing:} 
    When incoming requests are batched into bounded combinatorial instances ($BS \le 20$), exact branch-and-cut optimization using the open-source \texttt{HiGHS} solver~\cite{huangfu2018highs} exhibits high computational efficiency, maintaining negligible mathematical resolution times. However, attempting unbuffered single-task dispatch ($BT = 0.0\text{ s}$) does not eliminate latency under high load ($\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$): repeatedly instantiating optimization routines for individual requests induces coordinator CPU thrashing and forces solver invocations to queue up sequentially. This execution serialization negates zero-buffering assumptions and degrades optimization into a myopic, single-task greedy allocation.

    \item \textbf{Bounded Nature of Onboard Physical Task Execution ($T_{exec}$):} 
    Across all evaluated algorithms, arrival regimes, and workload distributions, the physical execution time required by the destination Satellite Edge Node (SEN) CPU cores to process the assigned workload ($T_{exec}$) remains stable and strictly bounded ($T_{exec} \approx 0.17\text{--}0.48\text{ s}$, reaching at most $0.65\text{ s}$ under massive payload transfers). This invariance proves that system saturation is never caused by insufficient raw onboard computational speed on individual satellites.

    \item \textbf{Ingress Queuing Dwell Time as the Dominant Bottleneck:} 
    System throughput collapse under elevated arrival rates ($\lambda \ge 6\text{ req/s}$) is governed almost exclusively by the ingress queuing latency ($T_{wait}$) accumulated in coordinator buffers prior to solver instantiation. In configurations relying on wide aggregation windows ($BS \ge 6$ or $BT \ge 0.4\text{ s}$), buffering dwell time systematically exhausts application deadline slack ($\Delta t_{slack} = D_r - [T_{wait} + T_{up} + W_{cpu,i} + T_{exec} + T_{down}]$), triggering cascade \textit{Deadline Exceeded} dropouts ($70\%\text{--}85\%$) before tasks can be scheduled and dispatched.

    \item \textbf{Operational Batching Archetypes (Small-Batching vs. Fast-Dispatch):} 
    To preserve application deadline margins while retaining combinatorial coordination, two distinct batching policies define the viable operating envelope:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textit{Controlled Small-Batching ($BS=2, BT=2.0\text{ s}$):} Establishes the minimal non-trivial combinatorial optimization horizon ($|R|=2$). Under sustained traffic ($\lambda \ge 4\text{ req/s}$), arrival bursts fill the two-request quota within $0.10\text{--}0.25\text{ s}$, flushing batches almost instantaneously. This bounds deadline-induced rejections to $\le 10\%$ in mixed workloads ($8\%\text{--}14\%$ in compute-heavy stress) and recovers peak throughput under heavy load ($54\%\text{--}67\%$ in mixed, $46\%\text{--}58\%$ in compute-bound).
        \item \textit{Reactive Fast-Dispatch ($BS=20, BT=0.05\text{ s}$):} Enforces an aggressive $50\text{ ms}$ timeout, delivering the lowest baseline response latency under light load ($0.50\text{--}0.54\text{ s}$ in mixed traffic). However, because it dispatches micro-batches with minimal combinatorial depth, candidate nodes suffer localized execution queue expansion under heavy load, trailing small-batching throughput by $12\%\text{--}25\%$.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Calibrated Objective Weight Tuning and Path-Bloat Prevention:} 
    The micro-tuning cross-sensitivity analysis identified the optimal multi-objective parameter set as $\alpha^* = 1000$ and $\gamma^* = 10$. Enforcing $\alpha = 1000$ under Time Mapping compels the branch-and-cut solver to prioritize transmission timeliness and deadline preservation, unlocking up to $+19.5\%$ higher completion rates over baseline weights. Concurrently, calibrating the orbital sunset penalty to $\gamma = 10$ completely suppresses eclipse-related drops ($0.0\%$) while strictly avoiding the severe path-bloat phenomenon observed when $\gamma \ge 1000$, which inflates end-to-end response time by over $55\%$ ($> 1.05\text{ s}$) due to circuitous multi-hop detours.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Algorithmic Archetypes and Workload Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:concl_workload_sensitivity}

The comparative evaluation against state-of-the-art distributed heuristics (\texttt{OrbitAware}, \texttt{DTS-base}, \texttt{DTS-Optimal}) and hierarchical optimization formulations demonstrated distinct operational boundaries governed by workload dimensionality:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Computation-Heavy Workloads ($\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0, 1, 0)$):} 
    Pure computational stress exhibits complete invariance across data payload distributions ($\boldsymbol{\alpha}$). Distributed online heuristics demonstrate superior resilience, sustaining $72\%\text{--}77\%$ success at peak load ($\lambda = 10\text{ req/s}$) with $100\%$ of rejections caused exclusively by physical core saturation (\textit{Insufficient CPU}). In contrast, hierarchical models collapse ($25\%\text{--}30\%$) due to multi-tier aggregation delay. Among centralized schemes, controlled small-batching ($BS=2$) successfully shifts $86\%\text{--}92\%$ of rejections back to physical CPU exhaustion, restricting deadline violations to just $8\%\text{--}14\%$ and out-performing fast-dispatch by up to $+25\%$ at peak load.

    \item \textbf{Communication-Heavy Workloads ($\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0, 0, 1)$):} 
    In data-intensive regimes, an algorithmic inversion occurs: optimization decisively surpasses distributed heuristics. \texttt{ILP-Hierarchical (Time)} achieves near-perfect completion rates ($99\%\text{--}100\%$) under light load and sustains $81\%\text{--}89\%$ at peak load under $80\text{ kJ}$, outperforming distributed heuristics ($77\text{--}78\%$). Rejections across all schemes shift entirely to transceiver and link capacity ($100\%$ \textit{Insufficient CPU+NET}), with pure CPU bottlenecks dropping to $0\%$. Furthermore, communication-heavy workloads exhibit high elasticity to constellation battery reserves: expanding battery capacity from $40\text{ kJ}$ to $80\text{ kJ}$ yields up to $+9\%\text{--}15\%$ throughput recovery in hierarchical models.

    \item \textbf{Mixed Multi-Service Workloads ($\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0.40, 0.35, 0.25)$):} 
    Heterogeneous multi-tenant scenarios reflect a fundamental operational trade-off between admission volume and latency control. When mission requirements demand raw task throughput, distributed heuristics maximize task admissions ($79\%\text{--}83\%$ at peak load) by eliminating ingress queue accumulation. When centralized SLA coordination and deterministic multi-hop scheduling are required, calibrated small-batching (\texttt{ILP-Centr [BS2, BT2, Time]}) provides the only viable mathematical configuration, achieving $82\%\text{--}92\%$ success under moderate traffic and compressing its end-to-end response time to $0.56\text{--}0.65\text{ s}$ as arrival bursts saturate the batch boundary.
\end{itemize}

\section{Critical Limitations of the Centralized Approach}
\label{sec:concl_limitations}

Despite the mathematical rigor and global optimality demonstrated by the proposed framework, deploying a pure centralized orchestrator in operational mega-constellations involves structural limitations:

\subsection{Ingress Buffering and the Batching Dilemma}
\label{subsec:limitations_batching}

Centralized mathematical scheduling requires aggregating incoming discrete requests into batches to instantiate the linear program~\cite{wolsey1998integer, boyd2004convex}. This creates an inescapable trade-off between optimization horizon and buffering latency.
While larger batch sizes allow the branch-and-cut algorithm to uncover superior combinatorial placement patterns across the fleet, the static wait time $T_{wait}$ directly consumes application deadline slack. For mission-critical real-time services characterized by tight sub-second latency bounds ($D_r \le 500\text{ ms}\text{--}1.0\text{ s}$), any artificial queuing introduced before solver execution severely degrades service availability~\cite{leyva2023satellite}.

\subsection{Single Point of Failure and Space Hazards}
\label{subsec:limitations_spof}

Relying on a designated orbital Master node introduces an acute Single Point of Failure (SPOF)~\cite{denby2020asplos}. Spaceborne edge computing operates in an exceptionally harsh environment subject to extreme thermal cycling, total ionizing dose (TID), and single-event upsets (SEUs) induced by cosmic rays and solar particle events~\cite{denby2019orbital, denby2020asplos}. If the central Master satellite suffers a radiation-induced latch-up, an onboard computer reboot, an attitude control anomaly, or requires an autonomous orbital collision avoidance maneuver, the entire task scheduling and forwarding pipeline across the constellation is immediately paralyzed.

\subsection{State Telemetry Overhead and Information Staleness}
\label{subsec:limitations_staleness}

To formulate an accurate mathematical representation of constellation constraints, the orchestrator must maintain an updated global state matrix containing processor queue lengths ($W_{cpu,i}$), residual battery reserves ($E_{rem,i}$), and active ISL bandwidths ($B_{u,v}$). Transmitting this telemetry across dynamic inter-satellite links incurs control signaling overhead that scales with the number of satellites ($\mathcal{O}(|S|)$)~\cite{survey_orbital_edge_2023}. 

Due to the extreme orbital velocities of LEO spacecraft ($\sim 7.5\text{ km/s}$), continuous relative motion alters line-of-sight angles and link propagation delays~\cite{chen2021isl}. Consequently, by the time state telemetry is gathered, buffered, and solved, the physical state of the constellation may have drifted. This state staleness risks rendering calculated schedules sub-optimal or infeasible mid-execution, necessitating reliance on emergency fallback routines.

\section{Future Research Directions}
\label{sec:concl_future_work}

The conclusions and limitations identified in this thesis pave the way for several natural extensions and advanced research trajectories.

\subsection{Two-Tier Hybrid Orchestration Architectures}
\label{subsec:future_hybrid}

To reconcile the trade-off between centralized optimality and distributed responsiveness, future architectures should implement the prescriptive deployment matrix synthesized in Chapter~\ref{chap:comparative_benchmark} via a two-tier hybrid framework:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Fast-Path Heuristic Layer):} Time-critical and compute-intensive requests ($\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0, 1, 0)$) are evaluated immediately at ingress access points using decentralized, zero-queuing heuristics (such as \texttt{OrbitAware} or \texttt{DTS-Optimal}), ensuring zero-buffering dispatch and eliminating deadline expiration.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Global Optimization Layer):} Data-intensive tasks ($\boldsymbol{\beta} = (0, 0, 1)$) carrying large payload transfers are directed to ILP solvers~\cite{zhong2025leo, jiang2025remote}. This tier exploits mathematical optimization where it demonstrably outperforms heuristics ($81\%\text{--}100\%$ success), maximizing optical link utilization and preserving fleet battery energy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Traffic-Aware Adaptive Batching via Reinforcement Learning}
\label{subsec:future_adaptive_batching}

The static batching thresholds ($BS, BT$) evaluated in this work could be replaced by an intelligent, dynamic batching engine. A lightweight Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent—such as a contextual bandit or Q-learning controller—could be deployed at the ingress gateway to dynamically modulate the timeout threshold $BT(t)$ in real time~\cite{fan2025satellite}:
\begin{equation}
    BT(t) = f\left( \hat{\lambda}(t), \, \min_{r \in Q} \{D_r - t_{arr,r}\}, \, \bar{W}_{cpu}(t) \right)
    \label{eq:adaptive_bt}
\end{equation}
By tracking traffic arrival surges ($\hat{\lambda}$) and the minimum residual slack time of buffered requests, the orchestrator could trigger immediate flushes during high-risk intervals and expand batch sizes during traffic lulls, optimizing the trade-off between scheduling efficiency and deadline preservation.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Distributed Reinforcement Learning (MADRL)}
\label{subsec:future_madrl}

To eliminate the centralized Single Point of Failure and bypass the communication overhead of global telemetry aggregation, decentralized Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning (MADRL) presents a compelling alternative~\cite{fan2025satellite}. Equipping each Satellite Edge Node with an autonomous actor-critic policy trained under Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) would allow satellites to make collaborative routing and scheduling decisions based solely on localized, one-hop neighborhood observations~\cite{fan2025satellite}. This model could approximate the global efficiency of ILP solutions while achieving the instantaneous decision speeds characteristic of distributed heuristics.

\subsection{Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) Prototyping on Space-Qualified Hardware}
\label{subsec:future_hil}

Finally, extending this simulation-based validation toward physical implementation requires testing the optimization pipeline on physical embedded hardware architectures. Deploying the \texttt{HiGHS} solver~\cite{huangfu2018highs} and the batching engine on radiation-hardened system-on-chip (SoC) architectures—such as the ARM Cortex-R52, Cortex-A53, or fault-tolerant space-grade RISC-V platforms (e.g., NOEL-V)—will provide empirical measurements of CPU instruction overhead, memory footprint, cache contention, and power consumption during real-time solver execution~\cite{denby2020asplos}.

\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:concl_closing}

The transition from passive bent-pipe relaying to active Orbital Edge Computing marks a significant evolution in space-based information systems~\cite{survey_orbital_edge_2023, denby2019orbital, tiansuan2023}. This thesis demonstrated that centralized Integer Linear Programming is computationally viable on spaceborne processing platforms, keeping solver resolution times negligible under bounded batches while destination node physical processing times remain stable and compact ($T_{exec} \approx 0.17\text{--}0.48\text{ s}$). However, the temporal latency imposed by request buffering in ingress queues represents a fundamental physical boundary that limits the scalability of centralized scheduling under high-frequency traffic.

By quantifying the operational trade-offs between centralized mathematical optimization, distributed hierarchical solvers, and autonomous heuristics across diverse mission profiles, this work provides architectural guidelines for designing the next generation of resilient, energy-aware orbital computing systems~\cite{icdcs_satellite_2025}.
